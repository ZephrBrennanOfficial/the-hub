# Objection Handling: "It's Too Expensive"

## The Real Meaning

When a prospect says "it's too expensive," they're rarely talking about price. They're saying one of three things:

1. **"I don't see the value yet"** — You haven't connected the cost to the outcome
2. **"I don't have the budget"** — A timing/cashflow issue, not a value issue
3. **"I'm not the decision maker"** — They need someone else to approve the spend

## How to Respond

### For #1: Value Disconnect

> "I understand — let's make sure we're comparing apples to apples. Can you walk me through what you're currently spending to solve this problem, including the hidden costs — time, missed opportunities, workarounds? Because what I've found is that the expensive option is usually the one that doesn't solve the problem."

**Why it works:** You're not defending your price. You're reframing their current "cheaper" solution as potentially more expensive.

### For #2: Budget Timing

> "That makes sense — budget cycles are real. Let me ask: if budget weren't a constraint, would this be the right solution for your team? If yes, let's figure out the timing together. I've helped teams phase implementations to spread costs across quarters."

**Why it works:** You isolate the objection. If they say yes, the problem is logistics, not value. Logistics can be solved.

### For #3: Wrong Decision Maker

> "Totally fair — this is a decision that affects multiple stakeholders. Who else should be part of this conversation? I'd rather get everyone in the room now than have you champion this alone and get a 'no' because someone didn't have the full picture."

**Why it works:** You're not asking them to fight your battle. You're offering to help them build the case internally.

## What NOT to Do

- ❌ Immediately offer a discount — you've just confirmed your price was too high
- ❌ List features to justify cost — features don't justify, outcomes do
- ❌ Get defensive — the objection isn't about you, it's about their uncertainty
- ❌ Use the word "but" — it erases everything you said before it

## The Meta-Principle

An objection isn't a wall. It's a window into what your prospect is actually thinking. The best salespeople don't overcome objections — they *understand* them.

# 04. Objection Roleplay & Coach's Correction Log

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Purpose:** A practice log documenting responses to 8 common B2B sales objections encountered during cold calling and initial discovery calls.

## 🎯 The Objection Handling Framework

Handling objections as an entry-level SDR requires moving away from reflexive defensive pitching and toward curious exploration. 

My objection framework follows four distinct steps:
1. **Acknowledge & Validate:** Neutralize tension with tactical empathy.
2. **Explore & Clarify:** Ask a targeted question to understand the root cause behind the knee-jerk reaction.
3. **Pivot to Value / Perspective:** Introduce a brief peer benchmark or alternative viewpoint.
4. **Low-Friction Next Step:** Close for a low-stakes discovery conversation.

Below are 8 real-world objection simulations formatted with my initial attempt alongside a **Coach's Correction** detailing senior-level execution.

## 🚫 Objection 1: "We already use [Competitor] and we're happy with them."

### Scenario Context
Cold call to a Sales Operations Manager regarding a CRM data enrichment tool.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"Oh, [Competitor] is a good tool! But our data accuracy is actually 98% compared to theirs, and we have live intent data. Have you looked at any alternatives recently, or would you be open to comparing pricing?"*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"Makes total sense, [Name]. [Competitor] is a established player and most teams we speak with use them. I’m certainly not asking you to rip them out today. 
> 
> Typically, when sales ops teams use [Competitor], they love the contact coverage, but they tell us they still struggle with 20-30% bounce rates on direct dials when prospecting mid-market executives. 
> 
> How has your team's connect rate been on phone numbers over the past quarter?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Defended features instantly, talked down the competitor, and pushed for a software switch on a cold call.
* **Senior Mastery:** Validated their current choice, avoided arguing, highlighted a specific known weakness of the competitor, and asked an insightful question about operational impact.

## 🚫 Objection 2: "We don't have budget for this right now / budget freeze."

### Scenario Context
Mid-call pushback during discovery with a VP of Marketing.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"I understand budget is tight! But our software actually pays for itself in 30 days by cutting your ad spend waste. Is there any chance you have discretionary funds left for Q3, or can we check back in Q4?"*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"I hear you completely, [Name]. With current market conditions, almost every leadership team we talk to has frozen non-essential software spending. 
> 
> When leaders tell us budget is frozen, it's usually because new tools feel like an added cost rather than something that offsets existing expense. 
> 
> We aren't asking for budget today. If we could show you how [Company] consolidated three separate marketing tools to save $2,000/month in net software spend, would it be crazy to take 10 minutes next week just to see the breakdown?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Argued with the prospect's reality, made unsubstantiated claims ("pays for itself"), and offered a weak delay ("check back in Q4").
* **Senior Mastery:** Empathetically validated budget freezes, reframed software from an *additive expense* to a *cost-consolidation tool*, and asked a No-oriented question.

## 🚫 Objection 3: "Just send me an email with some information."

### Scenario Context
Cold call opener response within the first 30 seconds.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"Sure thing! What’s your best email address? I’ll send over our PDF brochure and case studies right away, and then I’ll follow up with you next week!"*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"I’d be happy to send over some info, [Name]. But we have dozens of whitepapers and case studies—I want to make sure I only send what’s actually relevant to you so I don't clutter your inbox. 
> 
> So I know what to send: Are you currently more focused on reducing rep onboarding time, or improving your pipeline conversion rate this quarter?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Surrendered control immediately, became a "brochure sender," and guaranteed a low-converting follow-up email that gets buried.
* **Senior Mastery:** Agreed to the request graciously, then set a boundary by asking a quick two-option qualification question to convert the brush-off into a mini-discovery moment.

## 🚫 Objection 4: "Call me back in 6 months / next quarter."

### Scenario Context
Inbound follow-up or cold call brush-off where the prospect wants to kick the can down the road.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"No problem at all! I will put a reminder in my calendar to give you a call back in 6 months. Have a great rest of your quarter!"*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"I can certainly do that, [Name]. Usually when folks ask us to call back in 6 months, it’s either because this isn’t a priority right now, or you already have a full project roadmap for this quarter. Which one is it for you?
> 
> *[Prospect responds: "It's just not a priority until Q4."]*
> 
> Understood. If it’s becoming a priority for Q4, teams usually start evaluating options 60 days prior so they aren’t scrambling in October. Would you be open to spending 10 minutes now so you already have data when Q4 planning starts?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Accepted the delay passively without understanding whether 6 months was a genuine timeline or a polite brush-off.
* **Senior Mastery:** Friendly challenge to uncover the true reason, explaining the evaluation timeline logic to secure early mindshare before planning cycles start.

## 🚫 Objection 5: "I don't have time to talk right now."

### Scenario Context
Cold call interruption when the prospect picks up the phone while busy.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"Oh, I am so sorry to bother you! When would be a better time for me to call you back today?"*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"I completely understand, [Name]—I caught you completely out of the blue. I’ll be brief: I’m calling because we helped [Peer Company] cut their customer onboarding time in half. 
> 
> I don't expect to talk right now. Can I send you a 30-second calendar invite for Thursday at 9 AM, and if the timing still looks bad then, feel free to decline it?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Sounded overly apologetic ("sorry to bother you"), losing executive status immediately and forcing the prospect to manage calendar scheduling.
* **Senior Mastery:** Respected their time, delivered a 1-sentence value pitch, and made a ultra-low-friction calendar request.

## 🚫 Objection 6: "Is this a cold sales call?"

### Scenario Context
Prospect responds defensively as soon as they hear an SDR opener.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"Well, yes it is a sales call, but I promise I’m not trying to sell you anything today! I just wanted to introduce our company..."*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"It absolutely is a cold call, [Name]! I promise I don't have a 50-slide presentation ready for you. 
> 
> Look, I know you weren't expecting my call today. Give me 30 seconds to explain why I called your specific number, and if you think it's irrelevant, you can hang up on me. Fair enough?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Used cheesy line ("I promise I'm not trying to sell anything") which damages trust immediately because everyone knows it's a sales call.
* **Senior Mastery:** Embraced total transparency with humor, establishing authentic rapport and securing a 30-second window on the prospect's terms.

## 🚫 Objection 7: "We handle all of this internally in-house."

### Scenario Context
Cold call pushback to an agency or outsourced software solution.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"That's great that you have an internal team! But our agency specializes in this and can usually do it faster and cheaper than internal employees."*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"That makes sense—most well-established companies your size have internal engineering/marketing teams, and we certainly don't replace in-house talent. 
> 
> Usually, companies partner with us not to replace their internal team, but to handle specialized overflow tasks so their internal team can focus on core products. 
> 
> Is your internal team currently focused on core product builds, or do they have extra bandwidth for compliance automation projects right now?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Insulted the internal team's speed and cost efficiency, causing the prospect to get defensive about their employees.
* **Senior Mastery:** Validated the internal team, reframed the solution as *supportive overflow capacity* rather than a replacement, and asked a capacity question.

## 🚫 Objection 8: "Your price is too high compared to [Alternative]."

### Scenario Context
Objection raised during pricing discussion at the end of a discovery call.

### 🔴 My Initial Practice Attempt (Junior Rep Response)
> *"I understand! We are definitely a premium tool, but we have much better customer support and more features. Let me talk to my manager to see if I can get you a 15% discount."*

### 🟢 Coach's Correction (Senior Rep Execution)
> *"I appreciate you bringing that up, [Name]. Price is always a factor, and we are certainly not the cheapest option on the market. 
> 
> When prospects tell us our investment is higher than [Alternative], it’s usually because [Alternative] charges separately for onboarding, implementation, and API integrations, while ours is all-inclusive. 
> 
> Aside from the upfront sticker price, how are you currently evaluating the total cost of ownership including setup time and support over a 12-month period?"*

### 💡 Key Lessons & Differences
* **Junior Flaw:** Offered discounts immediately (destroying product value and margin) and made generic claims about "better support."
* **Senior Mastery:** Stood firm on pricing value, unpacked hidden competitor costs, and reframed price into Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) over a 12-month horizon.

# 01. Mock Discovery Calls & Self-Critique Log

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Purpose:** Simulated B2B sales discovery calls designed to practice active listening, rapport building, pain qualification, impact quantification, and securing clear next steps.

---

## 📌 Discovery Framework Overview

In these simulations, I practice executing a structured discovery conversation using a hybrid of **SPIN Selling** (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-Payoff) and **BANT/MEDDPICC** qualification criteria. 

Each simulation is followed by an honest, unvarnished **"What I'd Improve"** section where I analyze my performance, pointing out filler words, missed discovery paths, closed-ended questions, and areas where a senior AE/SDR would have dug deeper.

---

## 🎭 Scenario 1: Mid-Market B2B SaaS Discovery Call

* **Context:** Inbound lead request from a fast-growing 45-person B2B agency. The prospect requested a demo of **FlowScale CRM** (a workflow automation & CRM platform).
* **Participants:**
  * **Zephr Brennan** (Junior SDR / Account Executive)
  * **Alex Mercer** (VP of Sales at GrowthEngine Digital)

### Call Transcript

**[00:00 - 02:15] Upfront Contract & Rapport**

**Zephr:** Hi Alex, thanks for taking the time to connect today! How’s your week going over at GrowthEngine?

**Alex:** Hey Zephr, pretty busy as usual. We’re in the middle of Q3 planning, so calendars are pretty tight.

**Zephr:** I completely respect that, and I appreciate you making time. To make sure we respect your calendar today, I have us scheduled for 20 minutes. My goal for today isn't to blast you with a 50-slide PowerPoint deck. Instead, I’d love to learn a bit more about how your sales team is currently managing lead routing and client onboarding, share a quick high-level look at FlowScale if it makes sense, and by the end, we can decide together whether it’s worth setting up a deeper technical dive with our product team. How does that agenda sound to you?

**Alex:** Sounds good to me, Zephr. Let’s jump in.

**[02:15 - 08:30] Situation & Problem Discovery**

**Zephr:** Perfect! To kick things off, Alex, could you give me a sense of what’s happening in your sales workflow right now that prompted you to reach out on our website?

**Alex:** Yeah, so right now we're using HubSpot for CRM, but our deal handoff to the operations and delivery team is completely manual. When an Account Executive closes a deal, they manually fill out a Google Form, post in a Slack channel, and manually create tasks in Asana. Half the time, important client notes get dropped, and our onboarding team is delayed by 3 to 5 days just getting projects kicked off.

**Zephr:** Wow, that sounds like a headache. So the main issue is that AE deal notes aren't getting from HubSpot into Asana automatically?

**Alex:** Exactly. It's causing friction between sales and ops. Ops blames sales for bad notes, sales blames ops for slow onboarding.

**Zephr:** I see. And how long has this manual handoff process been in place?

**Alex:** Probably the last 8 or 9 months, ever since we scaled past 20 reps.

**[08:30 - 13:45] Implication & Business Impact**

**Zephr:** Got it. When an onboarding delay happens—like that 3 to 5 day lag you mentioned—what is the direct impact on the client experience or your team's operational bandwidth?

**Alex:** Well, clients expect momentum immediately after signing a $20k contract. When nothing happens for a week, they get anxious. We’ve actually had two clients cancel during the first 30 days this year specifically citing a chaotic kickoff. Plus, my AEs spend about 3 to 4 hours every week chasing ops status instead of prospecting.

**Zephr:** Two client cancellations in 30 days... if you don't mind me asking, what is your average annual contract value (ACV) for those clients?

**Alex:** Average ACV is around $25,000. So losing two early on is a $50,000 hit right off the bat, not to mention team morale.

**Zephr:** That’s significant. So beyond the $50,000 lost revenue, your AEs are losing ~4 hours a week on admin work. Across a team of 10 reps, that’s 40 hours of lost selling capacity every week—essentially losing a full-time rep's bandwidth.

**Alex:** Man, when you put it that way, it's pretty brutal.

**[13:45 - 18:00] Solution Alignment & Next Steps**

**Zephr:** FlowScale was built specifically to eliminate that friction. We integrate directly with HubSpot and Asana so that when a deal hits 'Closed-Won', a customized client onboarding space is created instantly with all AE notes pre-populated, and tasks assigned automatically. If we could eliminate that 3-5 day delay and save your reps those 4 hours a week, what impact would that have on your Q4 goals?

**Alex:** It would be huge. It would free up my team to focus on outreach and give our ops team peace of mind.

**Zephr:** Fantastic. Given that we have about two minutes left, I want to respect your time. Based on what you’ve shared, I think a targeted 30-minute tailored demonstration showing our HubSpot-Asana automation engine would be the logical next step. Are you open to scheduling that for Thursday afternoon at 2 PM or Friday at 10 AM?

**Alex:** Let’s do Thursday at 2 PM. Send over the invite.

---

### 🔍 What I'd Improve (Self-Critique & Junior Mistake Breakdown)

1. **Failure to Explore Decision-Making Process & Stakeholders (MEDDPICC Gap):**  
   * *What I did:* I focused heavily on the pain and immediately closed for a demo with Alex.
   * *What I missed:* I failed to ask who else needs to be involved in evaluating software changes. Does the Head of Operations need to sign off? Is IT required for security review?
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* "Alex, when GrowthEngine evaluates and purchases new workflow software, who besides yourself is involved in that evaluation? Should we invite your Head of Operations to our Thursday demo so they can see the Asana integration directly?"

2. **Surface-Level Problem Probing:**  
   * *What I did:* When Alex mentioned manual Slack/Google Forms handoffs, I immediately accepted it and moved to implications.
   * *What I missed:* I didn't ask *why* previous attempts to fix this failed (e.g., Zapier, native integrations, custom API).
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* "Have you tried solving this with Zapier or native HubSpot workflows in the past? What stopped those solutions from sticking?"

3. **Interrogative Cadence (SPIN Pitfall):**  
   * *What I did:* My transition from situation questions to implication questions felt a bit like a checklist audit rather than a natural conversation.
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* Use tactical empathy and labeling before digging into financial impact: "It sounds like the disconnect between sales and ops is creating a lot of friction for you personally as sales leader..."

## 🎭 Scenario 2: Creative Agency B2B Service Pitch Call

* **Context:** Discovery call with an E-commerce Founder who booked a call to discuss hiring a specialized growth marketing agency (**Apex Digital**) to scale paid acquisition.
* **Participants:**
  * **Zephr Brennan** (Agency Business Development Lead)
  * **Sarah Lin** (Founder & CEO of Lumina Skincare)

### Call Transcript

**[00:00 - 03:00] Framing & Goal Setting**

**Zephr:** Hi Sarah, great to connect with you! I’ve been following Lumina Skincare on Instagram for a few months now—congrats on the recent product launch!

**Sarah:** Thank you, Zephr! Appreciate it. Yeah, the launch went well, but now we're trying to figure out how to scale past this plateau.

**Zephr:** That’s exactly what I wanted to focus on today. Today is really about understanding where Lumina is currently at with paid customer acquisition, what bottlenecks you're bumping against, and seeing if our growth framework aligns with what you need. If it feels like a fit, we can talk about how an engagement would look. If not, I’ll happily point you toward some resources that might help. Fair enough?

**Sarah:** Perfect. Appreciate the transparency.

**[03:00 - 10:00] Current State & Pain Point Exploration**

**Zephr:** To get a full picture, Sarah, what are you currently doing for paid acquisition, and what’s driving the desire to bring on an outside agency right now?

**Sarah:** Right now, I’m running Meta ads myself, spending about $15,000 a month. Our Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) has crept up from $28 to nearly $48 over the last two quarters. Our return on ad spend (ROAS) dropped below 1.5x, and I just don't have the bandwidth to design creative, test new audiences, and manage campaigns while running product development.

**Zephr:** Got it. A CAC jump from $28 to $48 is significant, especially at $15k monthly spend. What happens to your business margin if CAC stays at $48 over the next two quarters?

**Sarah:** Honestly? We stop being profitable on first-time orders. We'd have to rely entirely on repeat customer lifetime value (LTV), which takes 60 to 90 days to realize. It severely constrains our cash flow and prevents us from ordering inventory for the holidays.

**Zephr:** Wow, so this isn't just about ad creative—it's directly impacting your inventory purchasing power and quarterly cash flow.

**Sarah:** Exactly. If we don't fix our acquisition efficiency by September, we can't fund our holiday inventory order.

**[10:00 - 15:30] Budget & Expectation Alignment**

**Zephr:** Thank you for sharing that context. To help me understand what success looks like, if we were to partner together, what target CAC or ROAS benchmark do you need to hit to unlock your holiday inventory funds?

**Sarah:** We need CAC back down below $32 and ROAS back above 2.2x.

**Zephr:** Understood. Typically, when we take over Meta paid media for DTC skincare brands, our retainer starts at $4,000/month plus a performance incentive once we hit target benchmarks. Is that aligned with the budget parameters you’ve set aside for an agency partner?

**Sarah:** $4,000 is a bit higher than I was expecting. We were hoping for closer to $2,500.

**Zephr:** I appreciate you being candid on that, Sarah. If we can demonstrate a clear roadmap that reduces your CAC back to $32—saving you ~$5,000/month in wasted ad spend—would you be open to reviewing a custom strategy proposal that shows how the math works?

**Sarah:** Yes, if the ROI math makes sense, I’d be open to seeing it.

---

### 🔍 What I'd Improve (Self-Critique & Junior Mistake Breakdown)

1. **Defending the Price Too Quickly:**  
   * *What I did:* When Sarah expressed budget hesitation ($2,500 vs $4,000), I immediately jumped to justifying the ROI math and pushing for a proposal.
   * *What I missed:* I didn't explore *why* she anchored on $2,500 or whether she had spoken to other agencies charging lower rates.
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* "I hear you, Sarah. $4k is a real investment. Curious—is $2.5k based on what you’ve paid agencies in the past, or is that what your current cash flow strictly allows right now?"

2. **Neglecting Creative Asset Velocity:**  
   * *What I did:* I focused purely on CAC and ad spend numbers.
   * *What I missed:* In modern e-commerce paid acquisition (Meta/TikTok), performance is 80% driven by creative iteration (UGC, video hooks). I didn't ask who produces her content or if she has existing video assets.
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* "Besides ad setup, who is currently creating your short-form video assets and ad angles? Do you have internal creators, or would you need our agency to handle full end-to-end creative production?"

## 🎭 Scenario 3: Outbound Follow-Up Discovery Call

* **Context:** Outbound follow-up to a Prospect who downloaded an eBook titled *"The 2026 B2B Cybersecurity Compliance Blueprint"*.
* **Participants:**
  * **Zephr Brennan** (Outbound BDR at SecureShield Software)
  * **Marcus Vance** (Director of IT & Compliance at Apex Financial)

### Call Transcript

**[00:00 - 01:30] Cold Opener & Permission**

**Zephr:** Hi Marcus, this is Zephr with SecureShield. I know I’m calling completely out of the blue—do you have 45 seconds for me to tell you why I called, and you can tell me if it’s worth continuing?

**Marcus:** *[Sighs]* Look, Zephr, I’m in the middle of preparing for a meeting, but go ahead quickly.

**Zephr:** Fair enough! I noticed you recently downloaded our 2026 Cybersecurity Compliance Blueprint on our website. Typically, when IT directors download that guide, it's either because they're preparing for an upcoming SOC 2 audit, or they're struggling to track compliance across remote employee devices. Which of those two is top of mind for you right now?

**Marcus:** Well, actually, we have a SOC 2 Type II audit coming up in November, and we're scrambling to gather compliance evidence.

**[01:30 - 05:00] Rapid Qualification & Uncovering Pain**

**Zephr:** Ah, SOC 2 audits are always stressful, especially when deadlines start creeping up. How are you currently gathering evidence across your cloud systems and endpoints?

**Marcus:** Right now? Spreadsheets, manual screenshots, and emailing department heads to confirm passwords and MFA settings are enabled. It takes dozens of engineering hours.

**Zephr:** Ouch. Gathering audit evidence manually with screenshots is brutal. How much time is your team spending per week just hunting down those compliance screenshots?

**Marcus:** Probably 10 to 15 hours a week across two Senior Systems Engineers. It’s taking them away from core infrastructure projects.

**Zephr:** Right, so senior engineers earning $120k+ are effectively acting as compliance administrators right now.

**Marcus:** Exactly. It’s a huge drain on resources.

**Zephr:** SecureShield automates 85% of SOC 2 evidence collection continuously in the background, eliminating the need for manual screenshots. If I could show you a 15-minute video walk-through or live demo next Tuesday morning on how we cut SOC 2 prep time from 3 months down to 2 weeks, would you be against taking a quick look?

**Marcus:** No, actually. Tuesday at 10 AM works. Send me a calendar invite.

---

### 🔍 What I'd Improve (Self-Critique & Junior Mistake Breakdown)

1. **Rushing to Secure the Meeting Without Deeper Discovery:**  
   * *What I did:* As soon as Marcus confirmed pain (manual SOC 2 screenshots), I immediately closed for the meeting.
   * *What I missed:* I secured the meeting (which is great for an SDR), but I failed to uncover key qualification criteria: Who is their audit firm? What cloud infrastructure are they running (AWS, Azure, GCP)? What budget is allocated?
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* Before locking in the time: "That’s awesome, Marcus. I’ll send that invite right over. To make sure our product team shows you the exact integrations you use—are you primarily hosted on AWS, GCP, or multi-cloud?"

2. **Using Closed-Ended Framing ("Would you be against..."):**  
   * *What I did:* I used Chris Voss's "No-oriented question" ("Would you be against taking a quick look?"), which worked, but the transition into the value proposition was slightly abrupt.
   * *Senior Rep Fix:* Smooth out the value anchor before asking: "Marcus, if we could hand those 15 hours a week back to your senior engineers while guaranteeing audit readiness, would it make sense to take a 15-minute look next Tuesday?"

## 📊 Summary Comparison: Junior Execution vs. Target Mastery

| Discovery Skill Area | Junior Tendency (Where I Am Now) | Senior Mastery (Where I'm Going) | Actionable Self-Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Questioning Technique** | Rapid-fire interrogation; reliance on surface situation questions. | Conversational, implication-led questions with tactical empathy. | Pause after answers; use "Tell me more about how..." instead of closed questions. |
| **Objection & Friction** | Defending price or pivoting too fast to product features. | Uncovering root cause of hesitation; anchoring value to quantified pain. | Ask clarification questions ("What makes $4k feel high?") before defending solution. |
| **Qualification Depth** | Stopping at single-stakeholder pain; missing process/budget authority. | Complete MEDDPICC mapping (Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Timeline). | Always ask: "Who else on leadership will be impacted by this decision?" |
| **Time Management** | Rushing to close the call once pain is identified. | Securing full context so the follow-up meeting is hyper-tailored. | Take 60 extra seconds to confirm tech stack, timeline, and audit dates. |

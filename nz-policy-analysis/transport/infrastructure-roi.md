# Transport Infrastructure: What Are We Getting for $6B a Year?

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ spends roughly $6B/year on transport infrastructure through the National Land Transport Programme (NLTP). This analysis tracks spending vs outcomes — congestion, public transport patronage, and road safety — to assess return on investment.

## What the Data Says

**1. Spending rose 75% over 7 years. Congestion got worse.** NLTP spend went from $3.6B to $6.3B. Auckland congestion went from +31% to +37% extra travel time. More road spending, worse traffic.

**2. Public transport hasn't recovered to pre-COVID levels.** Patronage is at 98% of 2019 — but population grew ~6% since then. Per-capita PT use is down ~7%. Meanwhile, the share of transport spending on PT dropped from 27% (2021) to 21% (2025).

**3. Road deaths are flat despite spending increases.** ~352 deaths in 2025 vs 378 in 2018. The deaths-per-100k rate improved slightly (7.6 → 6.6) but that's population growth, not road safety improvement. The absolute numbers are stubborn.

**4. Every major project blew its budget.** The four biggest transport projects all exceeded their original budgets by 17–50%. The total cost overrun across just these four projects exceeds $1.2B.

---

## Honest Questions

- If spending rose 75% and congestion worsened, is the problem under-investment or is it that we're investing in the wrong things?
- PT patronage is below pre-COVID levels on a per-capita basis while PT funding share is declining. Is this a deliberate mode shift away from public transport, or an unintended consequence?
- Major projects consistently overrun by 30–50%. Is this a procurement problem, a planning problem, or a risk-assessment problem?
- NZ's road death rate (6.6/100k) is worse than Australia's (4.2/100k) and nearly double the UK's (3.4/100k). What are those countries doing differently?

---

## Limitations

- Congestion data is from a commercial source (TomTom) and uses a specific methodology that may not capture all roads.
- NLTP spending includes maintenance and operations, not just new builds — comparisons over time reflect changing asset bases.
- Project cost overruns reflect scope changes, inflation, and ground conditions — not all overruns are project management failures.
- Road death data depends on reporting accuracy and may undercount serious injuries that later result in death.

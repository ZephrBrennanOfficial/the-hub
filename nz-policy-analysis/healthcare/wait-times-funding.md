# Healthcare Wait Times & Funding: Are We Getting What We Pay For?

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ's public health system is funded through general taxation and delivered through Te Whatu Ora (Health NZ). This analysis tracks funding growth against wait time outcomes to ask: more money — better results?

---

## What the Data Says

**1. Funding grew 69% in six years. Wait lists grew 116%.** Health spending went from $18.7B to $31.6B. But the number of people waiting more than four months for a specialist appointment went from 28,400 to 61,300. More money, worse outcomes.

**2. The 2021 restructure (creation of Te Whatu Ora) coincided with a sharp decline in performance metrics.** Whether the restructure caused the decline or merely failed to arrest it is debatable. The timing is not.

**3. ED performance has degraded steadily.** The "6-hour target" — patients admitted or discharged within 6 hours of ED arrival — dropped from 82% achievement to 63%. This is a frontline indicator of system stress.

**4. Per-capita spending rose 58%, but inflation over the same period was ~20%.** Real per-capita health spending grew by approximately 38%. The question is whether a 38% real increase should produce better results than it has.

---

## Honest Questions

- If funding grew 69% and wait lists grew 116%, where did the money go? Staff? Infrastructure? Administration? Contracting?
- The health system restructure merged 20 DHBs into one entity. Did consolidation deliver efficiency gains or create transitional costs that still haven't resolved?
- Is the wait time growth a funding problem or a capacity/throughput problem? These have different solutions.
- NZ spends less per capita on health than Australia (approx NZD $7,400 vs our $5,907). Is the gap explainable by structural differences, or is the system underfunded?

---

## Limitations

- Wait time data captures only those who have been referred and accepted — it doesn't count people who couldn't get a GP referral in the first place.
- The 2021 health restructure changed reporting frameworks, making some pre-2021 comparisons imprecise.
- Health spending figures include Vote Health but exclude ACC (which covers injury-related treatment separately).
- Per-capita comparisons across countries are affected by purchasing power and system structure differences.

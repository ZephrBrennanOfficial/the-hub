# Justice & Incarceration: What Are We Paying For?

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ's prison population has fluctuated significantly over the past decade, driven by policy changes, sentencing reform, and operational pressures. This analysis tracks spending, incarceration rates, recidivism, and asks whether the system delivers public safety or just costs.

## What the Data Says

**1. Cost per prisoner is ~$192,000/year — and rising.** That's more than most New Zealanders earn. The annual cost of incarcerating 10,480 people ($2.01B) exceeds the entire mental health budget for specialist services.

**2. Recidivism is declining but remains unacceptably high.** At 55%, more than half of all released prisoners are reconvicted within two years. The marginal improvement (57% → 55%) suggests rehabilitation programmes are having some effect, but the system is still primarily producing repeat offenders.

**3. Māori are 3x overrepresented in prisons.** Māori make up 17.8% of the population but 53% of prisoners. This ratio has been documented for decades and has not meaningfully improved. Whatever is causing this, current policy is not fixing it.

**4. Remand prisoners (unsentenced) are over a third of the prison population.** These are people held in custody awaiting trial or sentencing — legally innocent. At 3,590 people, NZ's remand population alone exceeds the total prison population of some smaller OECD countries.

---

## Honest Questions

- If recidivism is 55% and cost per prisoner is $192,000/year, are rehabilitation programmes actually funded at a level that could work, or is the system designed to warehouse people and re-receive them?
- Māori are 3x overrepresented. This has been true for 30+ years. What evidence-based interventions have actually been proven to reduce this disparity, and have they been implemented at scale?
- A third of prisoners are on remand — legally innocent. Is this a court capacity problem, a bail policy problem, or a risk-assessment problem?
- Norway's incarceration rate is 54/100k with a recidivism rate of ~20%. NZ's is 196/100k with 55% recidivism. What does Norway do differently, and is any of it transferable?

---

## Limitations

- Recidivism measures reconviction, not reoffending — many offences don't result in conviction.
- Ethnic disproportionality reflects multiple factors (socioeconomic, systemic, geographic) beyond justice system bias alone.
- International comparisons use different definitions and measurement periods.
- Remand figures fluctuate with court throughput and bail policy changes, which shift with government priorities.

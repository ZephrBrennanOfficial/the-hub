# Education: Funding Up, Achievement Down

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ's education system has received consistent funding increases over the past decade. Yet international benchmarks (PISA, TIMSS) show declining performance. This analysis tracks spending against achievement to ask the uncomfortable question.

## What the Data Says

**1. Funding per student rose ~32% over a decade. PISA scores fell across all three subjects.** Reading dropped 23 points, maths dropped 29 points, science dropped 22 points. In PISA terms, ~20 points roughly equals one year of learning.

**2. The decline predates COVID.** NZ's PISA scores were declining before 2020. COVID accelerated the trend but didn't start it. The structural issue is older.

**3. The achievement gap is large and persistent.** A 50-point gap in UE attainment between Decile 1 and Decile 10 schools means a student's postcode still strongly predicts their qualification outcomes.

**4. NZ's decline is steeper than the OECD average.** While many countries saw PISA declines, NZ's maths decline (-29) was larger than the OECD average drop (~-15). We're falling faster than our peers.

---

## Honest Questions

- If per-student funding increased 32% and scores declined, is the problem money or how it's being spent?
- The achievement gap by decile has been documented for 20+ years. What interventions have actually been proven to narrow it, and have they been scaled?
- NZ abolished decile ratings in favour of the Equity Index in 2023. Has this changed how funding is distributed, and is there early evidence it's working?
- Teacher supply is frequently cited as a bottleneck. What does the data say about class sizes, teacher retention, and the relationship between those factors and student outcomes?

---

## Limitations

- PISA and TIMSS are sample-based assessments with margins of error.
- Funding figures include all Vote Education spending (ECE, schooling, tertiary) — not just school-level operations.
- The decile-to-Equity Index transition makes longitudinal equity comparisons imprecise post-2023.
- International comparisons don't capture curriculum differences that affect what's being tested.

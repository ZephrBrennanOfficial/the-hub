# Tax: Who Pays, How Much, and Is It Enough?

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ runs a relatively simple tax system — no general capital gains tax, no payroll tax, no social security tax. This analysis examines who bears the tax burden, how it compares internationally, and whether the system raises enough to fund current spending.

## What the Data Says

**1. The top 3.5% of income earners pay 20% of all income tax.** The top 32.9% (those earning $70k+) pay 64.6% of all personal income tax. NZ's income tax system is progressive — the question is whether it's *sustainably* progressive if high earners leave.

**2. GST is regressive in practice.** The lowest-income quintile pays 14.2% of their disposable income in GST; the highest pays 9.1%. This is because lower-income households spend a higher proportion of their income on consumption. A 15% flat tax that's 14.2% for the poor and 9.1% for the rich is regressive by design.

**3. NZ's tax-to-GDP is below the OECD average.** At 31.2%, NZ collects less tax than the OECD average (34.0%). This isn't inherently good or bad — but it constrains what the government can fund. The $4.7B deficit in 2025 suggests the system doesn't raise enough for current spending commitments.

**4. No capital gains tax means property speculation is tax-advantaged.** NZ is one of the few OECD countries without a general capital gains tax. Rental property income is taxed, but capital gains on sale are largely untaxed (except for the bright-line test). This creates an incentive to invest in property over productive assets.

## Honest Questions

- If the system raises $4.7B less than it spends, what gives — raise more tax or cut spending? Which specific taxes or which specific programmes?
- The top 3.5% pay 20% of income tax. What happens to revenue if even a fraction of them leave (as the migration data suggests NZ-born citizens are doing)?
- GST is 15% flat. Would a lower rate on essentials (food, basics) make the system less regressive without a massive revenue hit?
- Is NZ's lack of capital gains tax actually driving investment away from productive enterprise and into property speculation? What does the data say?

---

## Limitations

- Effective tax rates don't account for transfers (Working for Families, benefits) which reduce the actual burden on lower-income households.
- International comparisons are affected by how social security systems are structured — countries with payroll taxes fund pensions differently.
- GST distribution estimates are based on survey data and household spending patterns.
- Corporate tax incidence (who actually bears the cost) is contested in economics.

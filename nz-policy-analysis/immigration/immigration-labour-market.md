# Immigration & the Labour Market: Who Builds NZ?

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ runs one of the most immigration-dependent economies in the OECD. This analysis tracks migration flows, workforce dependency, and the tension between economic growth and infrastructure strain.

## What the Data Says

**1. NZ's population growth is heavily migration-driven.** Natural increase (births minus deaths) is approximately 30,000/year. In 2023, net migration added 85,800 — meaning migration accounted for ~74% of total population growth that year.

**2. Key sectors are structurally dependent on migrant labour.** 41% of healthcare workers and 33% of construction workers were born overseas. Without migration, these sectors face immediate workforce gaps. This isn't a policy preference — it's structural dependency.

**3. Migration spikes correlate with infrastructure pressure.** The 2023 net migration of 85,800 coincided with renewed housing pressure, healthcare wait time increases, and transport congestion. Population growth at ~2.9% outpaced infrastructure capacity growth.

**4. The "brain drain" is the other half of the story.** NZ citizen departures have hit record highs — over 50,000 NZ-born citizens left in 2024. We're replacing departing Kiwis with arriving migrants. The net number looks healthy; the composition raises questions about retention.

---

## Honest Questions

- If 31% of the total workforce was born overseas, what does a "reduce immigration" policy actually look like without crashing the healthcare and construction sectors?
- The 2023 migration spike (85,800) was historically unprecedented. Was it a deliberate policy choice or an unintended consequence of post-COVID border reopening?
- NZ is losing citizens at record rates while gaining migrants. Is this population churn healthy or does it erode institutional knowledge and social cohesion?
- Population growth at 2.9% (2023) vs infrastructure spending growth — are we building houses, hospitals, and roads fast enough to keep up?

---

## Limitations

- Migration data uses Stats NZ's "12/16 month rule" methodology — arrivals counted as migrants if they're in NZ for 12 of 16 months. This creates lag in data.
- Workforce dependency figures use Census 2023 data, which may undercount temporary/seasonal workers.
- Population estimates are revised retrospectively — 2023 figures may adjust.
- Unemployment figures don't capture underemployment or labour force non-participation, which are significant in NZ.

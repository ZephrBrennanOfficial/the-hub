# NZ Housing Crisis: The Data Behind the Discourse

*Last updated: July 2026*

---

## The Core Problem

New Zealand's housing affordability crisis didn't happen overnight. It's the result of two decades of policy choices, supply constraints, and demand shocks. Here's what the data actually says.

## 1. House Price-to-Income Ratio

The most cited metric in housing affordability. It measures how many years of median household income it takes to buy a median house.

| Year | NZ Median House Price | Median Household Income | Ratio |
|------|----------------------|------------------------|-------|
| 2008 | ~$340,000 | ~$64,000 | 5.3x |
| 2013 | ~$425,000 | ~$72,000 | 5.9x |
| 2018 | ~$560,000 | ~$84,000 | 6.7x |
| 2021 | ~$820,000 | ~$98,000 | 8.4x |
| 2024 | ~$780,000 | ~$104,000 | 7.5x |
| 2026 | ~$745,000 | ~$112,000 | 6.7x |

**Context:** The international benchmark for "affordable" is 3x. NZ has been above 5x since 2003 and peaked above 8x in 2021.

**Key takeaway:** Prices have corrected ~10% from the 2021 peak, but the ratio is still more than double the affordable threshold.

## 2. Social Housing Waiting List

| Year | Households on Waiting List |
|------|--------------------------|
| 2017 | ~4,500 |
| 2019 | ~12,000 |
| 2021 | ~24,000 |
| 2023 | ~27,000 |
| 2025 | ~25,500 |
| 2026 | ~24,800 |

**Context:** The waiting list grew ~6x between 2017 and 2023. The slight decline since 2023 reflects both increased housing supply and tightened eligibility criteria — not necessarily fewer people in need.

## 3. Emergency Housing: The Hidden Cost

The Emergency Housing Special Needs Grant (EH SNG) tells a damning story:

- In 2018, NZ spent approximately **$20 million** on emergency housing
- By 2022, this peaked at approximately **$350 million**
- In 2025, spending remained above **$280 million**

That's taxpayer money going to motels — not homes. Every dollar spent on emergency housing is a dollar that didn't build a permanent solution.

## 4. Policy Timeline

| Government | Key Housing Policy | Impact |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| National (2008-2017) | Brightline test introduced (2015, 2-year period) | Minimal — speculative buying continued |
| Labour (2017-2023) | Brightline extended to 5 & 10 years, foreign buyer ban, KiwiBuild, Kāinga Ora ramp-up | Prices still surged to 2021 peak |
| National coalition (2023-present) | Brightline back to 2 years, foreign buyer ban reversed, housing acceleration | Prices correcting but affordability still poor |

**The uncomfortable truth:** Both major parties have failed to meaningfully address supply constraints — the root cause. The RMA reform debate has been going for 30 years.

## What the Data Doesn't Tell You

- **Regional variation:** Auckland's ratio is 8x+. Southland's is under 4x. National averages hide regional crises.
- **Hidden homelessness:** Couch surfing, overcrowding, and informal arrangements don't show up in official statistics.
- **Intergenerational wealth:** The bank of mum and dad is now the largest mortgage lender in NZ. Those without family support are locked out entirely.

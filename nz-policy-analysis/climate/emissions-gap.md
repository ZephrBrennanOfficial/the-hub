# Climate Policy: Are We Hitting Our Targets?

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

NZ has legislated emissions reduction targets under the Climate Change Response Act. This analysis tracks gross and net emissions against those targets and asks: are we on track, or is the gap widening?

---

|--------|-------------------|--------|

## What the Data Says

**1. Gross emissions have barely moved.** From 80.8 Mt (2015) to 78.2 Mt (2023) — a 3.2% reduction over 8 years. To hit the 2026–2030 target of 61 Mt/year, emissions need to drop ~22% from current levels in a very short timeframe.

**2. Agriculture is half the picture.** At 48% of gross emissions, agriculture is the largest sector by far. Any serious emissions plan must address agriculture — there's no way around it. But agricultural emissions have been politically untouchable.

**3. The ETS price peaked and declined.** NZU prices hit ~$75 in 2023 then fell to ~$64. A falling carbon price undermines the incentive to reduce emissions. If polluting is getting cheaper, the policy isn't working.

**4. Net emissions include forestry offsets.** The gap between gross (78.2 Mt) and net (54.8 Mt) is ~23.4 Mt of sequestration — mostly plantation forestry. When those forests are harvested, the sequestration reverses. Net targets can mask structural emission failures.

---

## Honest Questions

- If agriculture is 48% of emissions and has been largely exempt from serious policy, can NZ hit any target without addressing it?
- The ETS was designed to drive emissions down through price signals. Why has the price declined since 2023, and what does that mean for effectiveness?
- Forestry offsets currently account for ~23 Mt of our "net" emissions reduction. What happens when harvest cycles release that carbon back?
- The Climate Change Commission has said NZ is not on track for the 2031–2035 target. What specific policy gaps are they identifying, and what would close them?

---

## Limitations

- Emissions inventory data has a 2-year reporting lag — 2023 figures are the most recent published.
- Net emissions depend heavily on forestry accounting methodology (stock-change vs averaging).
- NZU prices reflect market dynamics including ETS settings, international units, and forest owner behavior.
- Sectoral splits use IPCC classifications which may differ from domestic policy categorisations.

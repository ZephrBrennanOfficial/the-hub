# NZ Cost of Living: Beyond the Headline Inflation Number

*Last updated: July 2026*

---

## The Problem with CPI

The Consumer Price Index is the most cited economic statistic in NZ — and the most misleading for understanding what ordinary people actually experience.

Here's why: CPI is an *average* basket. But housing, food, and energy costs don't hit everyone equally. A retiree on a fixed income and a young family paying $750/week on a mortgage experience inflation completely differently.

## 1. Headline Inflation vs Felt Inflation

| Period | Official CPI | Food Inflation | Housing/Utilities | Transport |
|--------|-------------|----------------|-------------------|-----------|
| 2022 | 7.2% | 10.3% | 8.5% | 12.1% |
| 2023 | 4.7% | 6.2% | 7.1% | 3.4% |
| 2024 | 2.9% | 1.8% | 5.2% | 1.1% |
| 2025 | 2.1% | 2.4% | 3.8% | 0.8% |
| 2026 (H1) | 1.8% | 1.2% | 3.1% | 0.4% |

**Key takeaway:** Headline inflation has cooled to within the RBNZ target band (1-3%). But housing and utilities remain stubbornly elevated — and that's where low-to-middle income households spend the largest share.

## 2. Real Wage Growth: The Number That Matters

Inflation alone tells you nothing. What matters is whether your income is growing faster than your costs.

| Year | Nominal Wage Growth | CPI Inflation | Real Wage Growth |
|------|-------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 2021 | 2.9% | 3.9% | -1.0% |
| 2022 | 4.8% | 7.2% | -2.4% |
| 2023 | 5.7% | 4.7% | +1.0% |
| 2024 | 4.1% | 2.9% | +1.2% |
| 2025 | 3.6% | 2.1% | +1.5% |
| 2026 (H1) | 3.2% | 1.8% | +1.4% |

**Context:** Real wages only turned positive in 2023 — after two brutal years of effectively getting pay cuts. The recovery is real but feels slow because households are catching up from a deep hole.

## 3. The Interest Rate Squeeze

Approximately 30% of NZ households have a mortgage. When the OCR went from 0.25% (2021) to 5.5% (2023), mortgage payments for many doubled.

- A $600,000 mortgage at 2.5% (2021): ~$2,120/month
- Same mortgage at 6.8% (2024): ~$3,940/month
- That's an extra ~$1,820/month — or $21,840/year — from the same household income

Even as the OCR has begun easing (down to ~5.25% by mid-2026), many households are still rolling off fixed rates into much higher payments.

## 4. Who's Hit Hardest?

The cost of living crisis is not evenly distributed:

- **Renters:** Rents rose 15-20% nationally between 2020-2025. No mortgage-free house to fall back on.
- **First-home buyers:** Caught between high prices and high mortgage rates. Both erode affordability.
- **Low-income households:** Spend 40-50% of income on housing vs ~25% for high-income households. Inflation in essentials hits hardest.
- **Mortgage holders:** Payment shocks from rate resets. Many cut discretionary spending to survive — which then hurts local businesses.

## The Honest Assessment

Inflation is cooling. Wages are growing in real terms. The economy is technically recovering. But "technically recovering" doesn't pay the bills for a family that's been running on fumes for three years. The gap between economic indicators and lived experience is the real story.

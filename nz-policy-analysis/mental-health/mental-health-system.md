# Mental Health: The System That Wasn't There

**Author:** Zephr Brennan  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Location:** Auckland, Aotearoa NZ

---

## Overview

In 2019, the NZ Government launched the "Access and Choice" mental health initiative with $1.9B over 5 years. This analysis tracks whether that investment produced measurable outcomes or just expanded bureaucracy.

## What the Data Says

**1. Primary access scaled massively — specialist access got worse.** People accessing primary mental health services went from 28,000 to 340,000 — a 12x increase. But the wait for specialist (secondary) mental health services went from 34 to 48 days. The front door opened wider; the corridor got longer.

**2. Suicide numbers barely moved.** 685 deaths in 2018, 672 in 2025. A 1.9% reduction over 7 years and $1.9B. Suicide is a complex, multi-factorial outcome — but if the goal was meaningful reduction, the data doesn't show it.

**3. ED mental health presentations rose 58%.** From 14,200 to 22,400. People are increasingly presenting to emergency departments for mental health crises — suggesting primary services aren't catching people before they reach crisis point.

**4. Workforce growth didn't match demand.** Service access grew 12x; workforce grew 26%. That ratio means more people are being seen by fewer professionals per capita. Access volume increased; service depth may have decreased.

---

## Honest Questions

- If primary access scaled 12x but specialist wait times got worse, is the system bottlenecked at the secondary/tertiary level?
- The $1.9B investment improved access numbers. But did it improve outcomes? What outcome measures were set, and were they met?
- Mental health-related ED presentations rose 58%. Is this because people can't access community services in time, or because community services don't have capacity for acute presentations?
- Workforce grew 26% while service users grew 1,114%. What does service quality look like under that ratio, and is it being measured?

---

## Limitations

- Mental health outcomes are influenced by many factors beyond health system performance (economic conditions, housing, social connection).
- Suicide data is subject to coronial process delays — 2025 figures are provisional.
- "Access" counts people who had at least one session — it doesn't capture whether treatment was completed or effective.
- Funding figures include both primary and specialist mental health, making it hard to isolate the Access and Choice initiative specifically.

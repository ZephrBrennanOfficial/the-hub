# Sample LinkedIn Posts

> These are example posts written in different founder voices. Each one sounds like a different person — because that's the job. Your posts will sound like *you*, not like these.

---

## 1. Marcus Rivera — Founder & CEO, PulseFlow (B2B SaaS)
**Voice:** Direct · Casual · Opinionated

---

I almost killed a feature last week that 40% of our power users rely on.

Why? Because our dashboard showed low overall usage. Looked like a dead feature. Easy cut.

But I talked to 3 customers before making the call. Turns out those power users run their entire weekly reporting flow through it. Kill the feature, kill their workflow.

Dashboards tell you what. They never tell you why. Only conversations do.

I almost made a $200K churn decision based on a graph. A 15-minute call saved it.

Talk to your customers. The data can wait.

---
**Why this works:** Short sentences. No fluff. Contrarian edge ("the data can wait"). Founder credibility from specific numbers. The hook stops the scroll because every founder has almost cut the wrong thing.

## 2. Priya Lakshmi — Founder, Lakshmi Creative (Marketing Agency)
**Voice:** Warm · Storytelling · Reflective

---

A client told me last month: "We love your work, but we went with the cheaper agency."

I said: "I understand. Can I ask — what made them cheaper?"

They paused. "They didn't ask about our goals. They just sent a quote."

And there it is. The cheaper option isn't cheaper. It's less work. Less thinking. Less questioning. Less friction.

I'd rather lose a pitch because I asked too many questions than win one because I asked none.

The right client doesn't want less friction. They want better outcomes. And better outcomes come from uncomfortable questions.

We lost that client. But the ones who stay? They stay because we ask the hard things.

---
**Why this works:** Storytelling format. Reflective, not aggressive. Warm but pointed. Uses a real (anonymised) client conversation. The lesson lands because it's honest, not preachy.

## 3. James Thompson — Director, Thompson Wealth (Financial Planning)
**Voice:** Professional · Trust-building · No jargon

---

Nobody talks about the boring part of wealth building.

Everyone posts about their portfolio returns. Nobody posts about the 3am panic when the market drops 8% and you're wondering if you should sell everything.

Here's what I tell every client: the strategy isn't the hard part. The strategy is a spreadsheet. Any advisor can build one.

The hard part is holding your nerve when every news headline is screaming at you to act.

The best investors I've worked with aren't the smartest. They're the calmest.

If your financial advisor has never talked to you about emotional discipline, you don't have an advisor. You have a salesperson.

---
**Why this works:** Professional but human. Builds trust by naming the uncomfortable thing nobody says. Calls out the industry without being aggressive. The closing line is provocative enough to drive comments.

## 4. Dale Whitaker — Owner, Whitaker Construction
**Voice:** Blunt · Practical · No-nonsense

---

Turned down a $400K contract this week.

Client wanted a fixed price on a renovation with no scope, no plans, and "we'll figure it out as we go."

That's not a project. That's a lawsuit waiting to happen.

Every builder I know who's gone under took on work they should've walked from. Not because they couldn't do the job. Because they couldn't say no to the money.

The cheapest lesson in construction is the one you learn by saying no. The most expensive one is the one you learn by saying yes.

Know your limits. Walk from the red flags. The right work comes if you're good at what you do.

---
**Why this works:** Short. Blunt. Real. No metaphors, no theory — just experience. Speaks directly to other tradies who've been in the same position. The $400K number makes the stakes real.

## 5. Anna Kim — Co-founder, Kindred (HR Tech Startup)
**Voice:** Honest · Vulnerable · Building in public

---

I hired someone last year who was perfect on paper.

10 years of experience. Worked at companies I admire. Charismatic in interviews. Checked every box.

They lasted 4 months.

Not because they weren't capable. They were. But they couldn't work in ambiguity. Every time something shifted — and in a startup, everything shifts weekly — they froze. They needed process, structure, predictability.

We had none of those things. We still don't. We're building the plane while flying it.

I learned: hire for adaptability, not just ability. The best resume doesn't survive chaos. The best person does.

Cost me 4 months and a lot of awkward conversations to learn that. Sharing so it doesn't cost you the same.

---
**Why this works:** Vulnerable founder story. Specific, not vague. Lessons shared openly. The "building in public" tone — honest about mistakes. Other founders relate because they've all made a hire they regret.

## What These Have in Common

- **Hook in the first line** that stops the scroll
- **Short paragraphs** — one idea per line
- **Specific details** — numbers, scenarios, real moments (not vague generalizations)
- **A point of view** — not "here's what I learned" but "here's what I believe"
- **No jargon** — plain English, the way people actually talk
- **A close** that makes you want to comment or share

## What These Don't Have

- Generic "thought leadership" that says nothing
- Buzzwords like "synergy," "pivot," "deep dive," "circle back"
- Long intros that take 4 paragraphs to get to the point
- Corporate speak that sounds like it was written by a committee
- Hashtags stuffed into every sentence

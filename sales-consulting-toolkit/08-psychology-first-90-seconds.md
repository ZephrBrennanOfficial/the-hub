# The Psychology of the First 90 Seconds

## Why the First 90 Seconds Matter More Than Your Pitch

Research shows people form a judgment about you within the first 90 seconds of an interaction — long before you've gotten to your value proposition. Here's what's actually happening and how to use it.

## What's Happening Under the Hood

### 1. Pattern Matching (0-10 seconds)
Your prospect's brain is rapidly comparing you to every salesperson they've ever met. If you open with "Hi, I'm X from Y company, I wanted to talk to you about..." you've already triggered their salesperson pattern — and the walls go up.

**Instead:** Open with a pattern interrupt.

> "Before I start pitching you anything — and I will, that's why I'm here — I want to understand if this conversation even makes sense for you. Can I ask you two questions first?"

**Why it works:** You've acknowledged the elephant in the room (you're selling something), but you've positioned yourself as someone who respects their time and wants fit, not just a meeting.

### 2. Status Signaling (10-30 seconds)
People calibrate their relative status within the first 30 seconds. If you're apologetic, overly deferential, or rushing to please, you've signaled lower status — and now everything you say is filtered through "they need me more than I need them."

**Instead:** Match their energy. If they're formal, be formal. If they're casual, relax. You're a peer with something valuable, not a vendor begging for attention.

### 3. The Curiosity Gap (30-60 seconds)
The human brain can't resist an open loop. If you can create genuine curiosity in the first minute, you earn the right to the next ten.

> "I noticed something in your industry that surprised me — can I share it? Most [role] I talk to are doing X, but the ones who are actually winning are doing Y. I'm curious which camp you're in."

**Why it works:** You've created a gap between what they know and what they want to know. Their brain will demand closure.

### 4. The Trust Anchor (60-90 seconds)
By now, they've decided whether to engage or disengage. If you've done the first three well, you've earned ~5 minutes of real attention. Use it to anchor trust with specificity.

> "I worked with a company similar to yours last quarter — same challenge with [specific problem]. They were doing [specific thing] and it was costing them [specific number]. I'm not saying that's you, but it's worth checking."

**Why it works:** Specificity = credibility. "I helped a company like yours" is vague. "They were doing X and it was costing Y" is proof you actually know what you're talking about.

## The 90-Second Audit

Record your next three cold calls or discovery meetings. Watch the first 90 seconds and ask:

- Did I trigger the "salesperson" pattern in the first 10 seconds?
- Did I match their energy or default to my script?
- Did I create curiosity or just deliver information?
- Did I use specificity or generalities?

Most reps fail this audit. The ones who pass it are the ones who book the second meeting.
